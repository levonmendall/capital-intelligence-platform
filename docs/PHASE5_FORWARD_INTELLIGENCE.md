# Phase 5 — Forward Business, Theme, Monetary and Currency Intelligence

The existing six-specialist committee now receives governed, point-in-time forward evidence covering strategic business economics, current market-cycle trends, structural demand chains, monetary-policy transmission and currency effects.

The business layer evaluates revenue exposure, demand, pricing power, capacity, incremental margins, market-share movement, capital allocation, customer and supplier concentration, and the degree to which expected benefits appear priced into valuation.

The market layer distinguishes broad earnings-supported trends from narrow, crowded or reversing moves using absolute and relative trend, breadth, earnings revisions, volume, leadership concentration, crowding and valuation expansion.

The structural-theme layer represents demand transmission through value chains, identifies capacity bottlenecks and potential next beneficiaries, and evaluates competing paths such as capacity expansion, substitution or weakening end demand.

The monetary layer treats QE, QT, rate changes and peak-rate conditions as conditional rather than mechanical signals. Policy motive, inflation, growth, financial stress, liquidity, real yields, credit spreads and market-implied expectations determine candidate-specific effects.

The currency layer evaluates dollar strength or weakness through translation, revenue and cost currency, debt currency, dollar funding, exporter margins, commodity invoicing, carry, FX volatility and hedge ratios. Dollar strength therefore may pressure unhedged foreign assets and dollar borrowers while benefiting some non-U.S. exporters with dollar revenue and local-currency costs.

These engines enrich the existing Macro, Market, Cross-Asset Forecast and Fundamental/Valuation specialists. They do not create a seventh specialist, authorize capital, lower any threshold or bypass evidence, robust edge, downside, liquidity, cost, portfolio construction or CIO-only authority. Execution remains paper-only.
